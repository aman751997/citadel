Every page explains one topic in plain words, with the real code, real numbers from real runs, the honest failure stories, and the questions interviewers actually ask — with model answers. Kept in your personal space on purpose: it is candid about what is production-grade and what is not.

## The project at a glance

**The business problem.** Hospitals accumulate paper: discharge summaries, lab reports, consultation notes. A doctor opening a patient's record has about ninety seconds to learn what medicines the person is on, what the labs are doing, and whether anything dangerous is going on.

**What the service does.** It reads every uploaded document and keeps four live sections of the record up to date: active medications, recent labs, a patient timeline, and critical alerts. Doctors are the end users of those cards. It is a Python service driven by RabbitMQ events from the Java patient-service.

**My responsibilities.** The pitch (page 1) says "I built the service" for the Python pipeline, and the chapters name things you did yourself: the span resolver and provenance fixes, the thinking-budget and model comparisons, the Postman measurements of the on-prem model, the recommendation sent to DevOps. The Java patient-service is the existing system of record. <StatusBadge kind="unverified" /> The text does not say which Java changes (webhook receiver, STOMP relay, page-map extraction) you wrote yourself versus a colleague. Confirm this yourself and rehearse one honest sentence about it before an interview.

## How to use it

1. Read pages 1 → 10 once, slowly. Say each "one-line answer" out loud. Then 13 for breadth.
2. Drill with page 14: cover the answer, five-minute timer, talk out loud. 8+ on the scorecard, three times, per drill.
3. Before an interview: page 1 (the pitch), page 9 (production readiness), page 12 (numbers), page 11 (question bank). Twenty minutes.
4. When a question stumps you in an interview, add it to page 11 the same evening. The bank is yours.

## Ground rules for answering

* **Lead with the number.** "55 seconds per document, 49–75 entities, 100% of spans located" beats "it works well".
* **Own the gaps.** Interviewers probe for honesty. "That part is a stub; here is what I would do" scores higher than pretending.
* **Say why, not what.** Every design here has a reason (and usually a bug that taught it). The reason is the answer.
* **Draw it.** Page 1 has the diagram. Practise drawing it in under two minutes.
* **Clarify → constraints → current → change → what breaks → verify.** The order for any design question (page 14).
